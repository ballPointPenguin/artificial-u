## **Course Title: Crossroads of Humanity: Climate, Culture, and the Great Transition**

**Academic Department:** Interdisciplinary Studies (jointly offered by Anthropology, Earth Sciences, and Ecology)
**Course Description:** This interdisciplinary course delves into one of the most transformative periods in human history: the transition from the Pleistocene to the Holocene epochs. We will explore the dramatic climatic shifts, particularly the Younger Dryas event, and their profound impact on global ecosystems, human populations, and cultural development. Drawing on insights from archaeology, paleoclimatology, paleoecology, and anthropology, we will examine how our ancestors navigated these environmental challenges, culminating in the "Neolithic Revolutions" – the independent origins of agriculture and sedentism across the globe – and the subsequent emergence of complex societies. This course aims to provide a holistic understanding of the intricate interplay between climate, environment, and the trajectory of prehistoric human cultural evolution.
**Prerequisites:** None. An introductory course in Anthropology, Earth Science, or Ecology is recommended but not required.
**Related Courses:** Quaternary Science, Environmental Archaeology, Human Evolution, Global Climate Change

### **Weekly Topics and Readings**

**Week 1: Introduction to the Pleistocene-Holocene Transition**

* **Lecture Title:** A World in Flux: Setting the Stage for a New Epoch
* **Topics:** Defining the Pleistocene and Holocene; overview of glacial-interglacial cycles; key environmental indicators and dating methods for the late Quaternary.
* **Readings:**
  * Ruddiman, William F. *Earth's Climate: Past and Future*. (Chapter 15: "The Last Glacial Maximum and Deglaciation")
  * Walker, Mike, et al. "The Global Stratotype Section and Point (GSSP) for the Base of the Holocene Series (Quaternary System)." *Episodes*.

**Week 2: The Last Glacial Maximum and its Aftermath**

* **Lecture Title:** Ice Age Earth: Human Adaptations at the Peak of Glaciation
* **Topics:** Global ice sheets and sea levels; megafauna and their ecological roles; human populations and cultural adaptations during the LGM.
* **Readings:**
  * Mithen, Steven. *After the Ice: A Global Human History, 20,000-5000 BC*. (Chapter 1: "The World of the Ice Hunters")
  * MacPhee, Ross D.E., and Hans-Dieter Sues (Eds.). *Extinctions in Near Time: Causes, Contexts, and Consequences*. (Selected chapters on megafaunal extinctions)

**Week 3: The Mystery of the Younger Dryas**

* **Lecture Title:** A Sudden Chill: Abrupt Climate Change and its Global Footprint
* **Topics:** Evidence for the Younger Dryas (YD) event (ice cores, ocean sediments, pollen); proposed mechanisms (meltwater pulse, Heinrich Events); global impacts.
* **Readings:**
  * Alley, Richard B. *The Two-Mile Time Machine: Ice Cores, Abrupt Climate Change, and Our Future*. (Chapter on the Younger Dryas)
  * Broecker, Wallace S. "Thermohaline Circulation, the Achilles Heel of Our Climate System: Will Man-Induced CO2 Accumulation Amplify a Natural Instability?" *Science*.

**Week 4: The Younger Dryas and Human Societies: Global Perspectives**

* **Lecture Title:** Responding to Crisis: Human Responses to the Younger Dryas Across Continents
* **Topics:** Archaeological evidence for human responses to the YD in different regions (e.g., Near East, North America, Europe); subsistence strategies and mobility patterns.
* **Readings:**
  * Goring-Morris, A. Nigel, and Anna Belfer-Cohen (Eds.). *The Younger Dryas and the Near Eastern Neolithic: Perspectives from the Southern Levant*. (Selected papers)
  * Haynes, C. Vance. "The Clovis-Folsom-Younger Dryas Connection." *Current Research in the Pleistocene*.

**Week 5: Foraging in a Changing World: Epipaleolithic Adaptations**

* **Lecture Title:** Beyond Hunting and Gathering: Intensification and Resource Specialization
* **Topics:** Broad-spectrum revolution; development of more complex foraging strategies; early sedentism among hunter-gatherers (e.g., Natufian culture).
* **Readings:**
  * Flannery, Kent V. "The Origins of Agriculture." *Annual Review of Anthropology*.
  * Bar-Yosef, Ofer. "The Natufian Culture in the Levant: Threshold to the Origins of Agriculture." *Evolutionary Anthropology*.

**Week 6: The Neolithic Revolution: Origins of Agriculture (Near East)**

* **Lecture Title:** The Fertile Crescent Awakens: Domestication and the Dawn of Farming
* **Topics:** Independent origins of agriculture in the Near East; domestication of wheat, barley, and early livestock; key sites like Göbekli Tepe, Çatalhöyük, and Jericho.
* **Readings:**
  * Zeder, Melinda A. "Domestication and Early Agriculture in the Near East: Precursor to Civilization." *Annual Review of Anthropology*.
  * Schmidt, Klaus. *Göbekli Tepe: Ten Thousand-Year-Old Temples of the Stone Age*.

**Week 7: The Neolithic Revolution: Independent Origins Around the World**

* **Lecture Title:** A Global Transformation: Diverse Pathways to Agriculture
* **Topics:** Independent domestication centers in Mesoamerica (maize, beans), South America (potatoes, quinoa), East Asia (rice, millet), and Africa (sorghum, millet).
* **Readings:**
  * Smith, Bruce D. *The Emergence of Agriculture*. (Selected chapters on specific regional developments)
  * Diamond, Jared. *Guns, Germs, and Steel*. (Chapter 5: "History's Haves and Have-Nots")

**Week 8: The Ecological Impact of Early Agriculture**

* **Lecture Title:** Reshaping Landscapes: The Anthropocene's Early Footprint
* **Topics:** Deforestation and land clearance; irrigation and water management; changes in biodiversity; the co-evolution of humans and their domesticated landscapes.
* **Readings:**
  * Ruddiman, William F. *Plows, Plagues, and Petroleum: How Humans Took Control of Climate*. (Chapter on early agriculture)
  * Fuller, Dorian Q., et al. "The Archaeology of Agricultural Origins: New Perspectives from the Early Holocene." *Current Anthropology*.

**Week 9: Social and Cultural Transformations in the Early Neolithic**

* **Lecture Title:** Life in the Village: Community, Kinship, and the First Farmers
* **Topics:** Development of settled village life; changes in social organization and kinship structures; early evidence of communal storage and ritual practices.
* **Readings:**
  * Scott, James C. *Against the Grain: A Deep History of the Earliest States*. (Introduction and relevant chapters)
  * Hodder, Ian. *The Leopard's Tale: Reveiling the Mysteries of Çatalhöyük*.

**Week 10: The Bioarchaeology of the Neolithic Transition**

* **Lecture Title:** Health and Hardship: What Skeletons Tell Us About Early Farmers
* **Topics:** Paleodietary analysis (stable isotopes); dental health and disease patterns; skeletal indicators of stress and workload; population growth.
* **Readings:**
  * Cohen, Mark Nathan. *Health and the Rise of Civilization*. (Selected chapters)
  * Larsen, Clark Spencer. *Bioarchaeology: Interpreting Behavior from the Human Skeleton*. (Relevant chapters on agricultural impacts)

**Week 11: The Cognitive and Ideological Shifts of the Neolithic**

* **Lecture Title:** Mind, Matter, and Meaning: New Ways of Seeing the World
* **Topics:** Changes in cosmology and worldview; the role of ritual and symbolism in early agricultural societies; development of new forms of material culture as expressions of identity.
* **Readings:**
  * Cauvin, Jacques. *The Birth of the Gods and the Origins of Agriculture*. (Selected chapters)
  * Pearson, Mike Parker, and Colin Richards (Eds.). *Architecture and Order: Approaches to Social Space*. (Relevant chapters on Neolithic built environments)

**Week 12: Legacy of the Great Transition: The Foundations of Civilization**

* **Lecture Title:** From Village to City: The Long-Term Consequences of the Neolithic Revolution
* **Topics:** Population growth and increasing density; emergence of craft specialization and trade networks; the initial steps towards urbanism and state formation; ongoing debates about the "progress" of the Neolithic.
* **Readings:**
  * Algaze, Guillermo. *Mesopotamian Dark Age Revisited: Archaeology and the End of the Prehistoric World*. (Introduction)
  * Harari, Yuval Noah. *Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind*. (Chapter on the Agricultural Revolution)
